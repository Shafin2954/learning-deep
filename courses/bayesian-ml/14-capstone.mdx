## The project

Build a small probabilistic-inference toolkit of your own and use it to analyse one real grouped
dataset end to end — for example per-store sales, per-hospital outcomes, per-campaign conversion
rates, or exam scores nested in schools. You will write the samplers yourself (Metropolis–Hastings,
Gibbs, and HMC), the diagnostics yourself (rank-normalised split R-hat, bulk and tail ESS, MCSE),
and a Gaussian-process regressor yourself; then rebuild the same hierarchical model in a
probabilistic programming language and show that your from-scratch answers agree with it to within
Monte Carlo error. The deliverable is the toolkit, a reproducible analysis, and a short report that a
sceptical reader could check. Agreement with the PPL is not the goal by itself — the goal is that
you can explain every number in both.

## Hard constraints (no vibe coding)

- **No AI assistants** for the code, the model choices, or the report.
- **From scratch, with NumPy/SciPy only** (SciPy for densities and special functions, not for
  optimisation of the posterior or sampling): the MH sampler, the Gibbs sampler, the HMC sampler
  with leapfrog and dual-averaging step-size adaptation, R-hat, ESS, MCSE, PSIS-LOO, and the GP.
- **PyMC or NumPyro is allowed only for the "rebuild" step** and for cross-checking. ArviZ is
  allowed only to *verify* your own diagnostics, never to replace them.
- **No `sklearn.gaussian_process`** in the GP you submit (you may use it in a test to verify yours).
- **Every number in the report comes from a script in the repository.** No pasted values.
- **Fixed seeds; the full analysis reproduces exactly** from one command.

## What it must contain (and the chapter each tests)

1. **A model written down before any fitting**: the generative story, priors with a one-line
   justification each, and a prior predictive simulation showing those priors produce plausible
   data — and at least one prior you rejected because its prior predictive was absurd. (ch. 1, ch. 8)
2. **A conjugate baseline** for a simplified version of the problem (e.g. Normal–Normal with known
   variances, or Beta–Binomial per group) solved in closed form, used later as a test oracle for
   your samplers. (ch. 2)
3. **A Laplace approximation and an importance-sampling estimate** of the posterior and evidence for
   one non-conjugate sub-model, with Kish ESS and Pareto $\hat k$ reported, and a sentence on
   whether you would trust each. (ch. 3)
4. **A random-walk Metropolis sampler** on the full hierarchical model, with proposal scale tuned
   during warmup and then frozen, and its acceptance rate and ESS per evaluation reported. (ch. 4)
5. **A Gibbs sampler** for the same model using derived full conditionals (Metropolis-within-Gibbs
   for any non-conjugate block), with the derivation of each conditional in the report. (ch. 5)
6. **An HMC sampler** with hand-derived or autograd gradients, leapfrog integration, dual-averaging
   step-size adaptation, and a diagonal mass matrix adapted in warmup; divergences counted. (ch. 6)
7. **Your own diagnostics module**: rank-normalised split R-hat, folded R-hat, bulk-ESS, tail-ESS,
   MCSE for the mean and for a quantile, and E-BFMI — each verified against ArviZ to at least three
   significant figures on the same draws. (ch. 7)
8. **The same model in a PPL** (PyMC or NumPyro), with posterior summaries compared parameter by
   parameter against your three samplers in a single table of means, sds and MCSEs. (ch. 8)
9. **Model checking and comparison**: at least two posterior predictive checks with statistics the
   model does not directly fit, a LOO-PIT calibration check, and a PSIS-LOO comparison of the
   hierarchical model against complete pooling and no pooling, with elpd differences and their
   standard errors, plus any observations with $\hat k > 0.7$ named and investigated. (ch. 9)
10. **Centred and non-centred parameterisations** of the hierarchy, with divergences, ESS of the
    group-scale parameter, and the decision of which to keep — justified by how informative your
    data is per group. (ch. 10)
11. **A variational approximation** (mean-field ADVI written yourself in PyTorch or NumPy), with its
    posterior means and sds compared to HMC, the variance underestimation quantified, and PSIS
    $\hat k$ used to judge whether the approximation is usable. (ch. 11)
12. **A Gaussian-process regressor from scratch** applied to one continuous covariate or a time
    trend in the same dataset: Cholesky-based posterior, hyperparameters by maximising the log
    marginal likelihood with an analytic gradient, held-out interval coverage, and a comparison to
    the parametric model on held-out log predictive density. (ch. 12)
13. **One uncertainty-aware prediction that someone would act on**, with the decision rule derived
    from an explicit loss function (posterior mean, median, or a quantile — say which and why).
    (ch. 1, ch. 13)

## Acceptance criteria

- **Samplers agree.** On the conjugate baseline, every from-scratch sampler's posterior mean is
  within 3 MCSE of the closed-form answer. On the full model, MH, Gibbs, HMC and the PPL agree on
  every reported parameter mean to within 3 combined MCSE.
- **Converged, and you can prove it.** Every reported parameter has rank-normalised R-hat below
  1.01 and bulk- and tail-ESS above 400 for the final HMC and PPL runs; your diagnostics match
  ArviZ to three significant figures.
- **Efficiency is measured, not asserted.** A table reports ESS per gradient (or per log-density
  evaluation) for MH, Gibbs and HMC, and the report explains the ranking.
- **No silent pathology.** The final HMC/PPL run has zero divergences, or the report shows they are
  harmless by comparing against a run with a smaller step size.
- **Honest model comparison.** The chosen model is supported by an elpd difference larger than two
  standard errors, or the report says the comparison is a tie and explains how it chose anyway.
- **Calibrated predictions.** The GP's and the hierarchical model's 90% predictive intervals cover
  between 85% and 95% of held-out points.
- **VI is judged, not trusted.** The report states the ratio of VI to HMC posterior sd for every
  group-level parameter and a $\hat k$ verdict.
- **Reproducible.** One command regenerates every table and figure in the report from raw data.

## Skills checklist (tick before you claim done)

- [ ] I can write a generative model and justify each prior with a prior predictive simulation.
- [ ] I can derive a conjugate posterior and its posterior predictive in closed form.
- [ ] I can explain why the evidence integral is the hard part, and when Laplace or importance sampling is enough.
- [ ] I can derive the Metropolis–Hastings acceptance ratio from detailed balance and tune a proposal.
- [ ] I can derive full conditionals and build a Gibbs sampler, including Metropolis-within-Gibbs steps.
- [ ] I can implement HMC with leapfrog, step-size adaptation and a mass matrix, and explain a divergence.
- [ ] I can compute rank-normalised R-hat, bulk/tail ESS and MCSE from scratch and say what each catches.
- [ ] I can express a model in a PPL and read every column of its summary table.
- [ ] I can design a posterior predictive check that could actually fail.
- [ ] I can compute PSIS-LOO, read $\hat k$, and compare models with an elpd difference and its SE.
- [ ] I can choose between centred and non-centred parameterisations from the data, not by habit.
- [ ] I can derive the ELBO, implement ADVI, and quantify how much VI underestimates uncertainty.
- [ ] I can fit a GP with Cholesky and learn its hyperparameters from the marginal likelihood.
- [ ] I can turn a posterior into a decision using an explicit loss function.
