## The project

Build a small numerical library in pure NumPy and use it to solve five machine-learning problems
end to end. The library has two halves. The linear-algebra half has a pivoted LU solver, Cholesky, Householder QR, least squares, power iteration and a randomized SVD. The optimisation half has gradient descent with line search, momentum and Nesterov, Adam and AdamW, Newton, L-BFGS, conjugate gradient, projected and proximal gradient, and a tiny reverse-mode autodiff that produces the gradients the optimisers consume. Every gradient is checked, every solver is compared against a trusted library, and every claim about speed is made in counted operations, not seconds. The deliverable is the library, a test suite, and a report that explains *why* each solver behaved the way it did on each problem. A table of numbers with no explanation does not count.

## Hard constraints (no vibe coding)

- **No AI assistants** for the code, the derivations, or the report.
- **NumPy only inside the library.** No `scipy.linalg`, `scipy.optimize`, `scipy.sparse.linalg`,
  sklearn, PyTorch or JAX in the solver code. They are allowed **only in tests**, to check your
  answers.
- **Never form an explicit inverse** to solve a system. Every solve goes through a factorization or
  an iterative method you wrote.
- **Every analytic gradient passes a central-difference check** before any solver uses it, with
  the step size and relative-error metric from ch. 8. Your autodiff must agree with your
  hand-derived gradients on every loss.
- **One interface for all optimisers**: `minimize(f, grad, x0, **opts) -> (x, history)`, where
  `history` records loss, gradient norm and cumulative gradient evaluations per iteration.
- **Every design choice is justified** by a measurement or a theorem from the course: step sizes,
  memory sizes, stopping rules, damping.

## What it must contain (and the chapter each tests)

1. **Vector utilities**: norms, cosine, Mahalanobis distance via Cholesky, and modified Gram–Schmidt
   with a measured loss of orthogonality against Householder. (ch. 1, ch. 3)
2. **A subspace toolkit** for any matrix: bases for the four fundamental subspaces, rank, and the
   orthogonal projector onto the column space, with $P^2 = P$ and $P^\top = P$ verified. (ch. 2)
3. **Linear solvers**: LU with partial pivoting, Cholesky, and Householder QR, each with forward
   and back substitution. Least squares three ways (normal equations, QR, SVD) with a table of
   error against condition number from $10^2$ to $10^{12}$. (ch. 3)
4. **Spectral tools**: power iteration with deflation and shift-and-invert for eigenvalues, and a
   randomized SVD with oversampling and power iterations, all checked against `np.linalg`. (ch. 4–5)
5. **PCA two ways** (covariance eigendecomposition and SVD of the centred data) agreeing to
   $10^{-8}$ on well-conditioned data. Plus a demonstration of where the covariance route loses
   accuracy, and probabilistic PCA with the noise variance equal to the mean discarded eigenvalue. (ch. 6)
6. **A positive-definiteness module**: a Cholesky-based PD test, nearest-PSD projection by
   eigenvalue clipping, and Ledoit–Wolf shrinkage for a covariance with $n < d$. (ch. 7)
7. **A reverse-mode autodiff** supporting `+ - * / @`, `exp`, `log`, `tanh` and `sum`, which
   produces the gradients for problems 11–13 and a Hessian–vector product by double
   differentiation. (ch. 8)
8. **Convexity checks**: for each loss you optimise, a numerical certificate of convexity on
   random segments (or a counterexample), and the smoothness and strong-convexity constants $L$ and
   $\mu$ estimated from Hessian eigenvalues. (ch. 9)
9. **First-order solvers**: GD with fixed step $1/L$ and with Armijo backtracking, heavy-ball
   momentum with the tuned optimal parameters, Nesterov, AdaGrad, RMSProp, Adam and AdamW. Each
   is matched step for step against `torch.optim` in a test, to $10^{-6}$. (ch. 10–11)
10. **Second-order solvers**: damped Newton with Hessian modification, BFGS with a Wolfe line
    search, L-BFGS (two-loop recursion), and conjugate gradient with Jacobi preconditioning. (ch. 12)
11. **Problem A, linear regression on an ill-conditioned design** ($\kappa \ge 10^6$): closed form
    via QR, GD before and after feature standardisation, CG, and L-BFGS, with iteration counts
    compared to the rates predicted by $\kappa$. (ch. 3, 10, 12)
12. **Problem B, logistic regression**: Newton/IRLS, L-BFGS, and Adam, matching
    `sklearn.linear_model.LogisticRegression` (same penalty) in log-loss to $10^{-6}$. (ch. 8, 12)
13. **Problem C, a two-layer MLP** on a synthetic nonlinear task, trained with your autodiff and
    your Adam/AdamW, with a learning-rate warmup, and a run that demonstrates a saddle or
    plateau. (ch. 8, 11)
14. **Problem D, constrained**: the lasso solved with ISTA and FISTA (iteration counts compared),
    and a small SVM solved through its dual with complementary slackness verified (support vectors
    = nonzero multipliers), matching `sklearn.svm.SVC`. (ch. 13)
15. **Problem E, numerics**: stable log-sum-exp, softmax and BCE-with-logits used in every loss
    above, Welford variance, Kahan summation, and a float16-vs-bfloat16 overflow demonstration. Each
    is paired with the naive version that fails. (ch. 14)

## Acceptance criteria

- **Correct linear algebra.** Your solvers match NumPy/SciPy to a relative error of $10^{-10}$ on
  well-conditioned systems, and your least-squares table shows normal equations failing near
  $\kappa \approx 10^8$ while QR does not.
- **Gradients are proven.** Every analytic and autodiff gradient passes the central-difference check
  with relative error below $10^{-6}$. A deliberately broken gradient in the test suite is caught.
- **Rates match theory.** For GD and heavy ball on a quadratic with known $\kappa$, the measured
  linear convergence factor is within 5% of $(\kappa-1)/(\kappa+1)$ and
  $(\sqrt\kappa-1)/(\sqrt\kappa+1)$ respectively. Newton shows quadratic convergence (correct
  digits at least double per step) near the optimum.
- **Second-order solvers pay off where they should.** On Problems A and B, L-BFGS uses fewer
  gradient evaluations than tuned GD, and the report explains the cost per iteration that
  makes Newton unattractive at large $d$.
- **Constrained solutions certify themselves.** KKT residuals (stationarity, feasibility,
  complementary slackness) are below $10^{-6}$ for the SVM. FISTA reaches the lasso tolerance in
  fewer iterations than ISTA.
- **No silent numerics.** No NaN or inf appears in any run. Each of your naive-vs-stable pairs
  shows the naive version producing a wrong or non-finite answer on a stated input.
- **Reproducible.** One command runs the test suite and regenerates every table in the report.

- [ ] I can find the four fundamental subspaces of a matrix and use them to say when $Ax=b$ has a solution.
- [ ] I can implement LU, Cholesky and QR, and say which to use and why normal equations are dangerous.
- [ ] I can compute eigenvalues by power iteration and explain what the spectral gap controls.
- [ ] I can prove Eckart–Young numerically and use a truncated or randomized SVD.
- [ ] I can derive PCA two ways and say when to standardise.
- [ ] I can test positive definiteness five ways and repair a matrix that should be PSD but isn't.
- [ ] I can derive gradients with the differential method and build reverse-mode autodiff.
- [ ] I can prove a loss is convex and read off $L$, $\mu$ and $\kappa$.
- [ ] I can predict gradient descent's rate from $\kappa$ and fix it with preconditioning.
- [ ] I can derive the heavy-ball optimal parameters and Adam's bias correction, and tell L2 from decoupled weight decay.
- [ ] I can implement Newton, BFGS, L-BFGS and CG, and say when each is worth its cost.
- [ ] I can write KKT conditions, derive a dual, and read complementary slackness off a solution.
- [ ] I can spot catastrophic cancellation and overflow, and replace each with a stable formula.
