## The project

You build three different "image makers" from their equations, train them on the same pictures,
and then judge them fairly side by side. If you can do that, you understand the course.

You will implement three generative models — a VAE, a DDPM, and a simple GAN — trained on
the same small image dataset (MNIST, FashionMNIST, or CIFAR-10). You will evaluate all three
with FID or KID and write a structured tradeoff analysis comparing sample quality, training
stability, exact likelihood, and sampling speed. The goal is not to match state-of-the-art
numbers; it is to prove you can build these systems from their mathematical definitions.

> **Picture it:** a cooking test where you bake the same cake three ways — oven, steam, and
> frying pan — from the same flour, then hold a blind tasting. The point is to know *why* each
> method gives the cake it does, not to beat a professional bakery.

Start with MNIST: it trains in minutes on a CPU, and its 10 digit classes make mode coverage
easy to check by eye. Move to CIFAR-10 only once everything works.

## Hard constraints (no vibe coding)

The rules make sure the core ideas pass through your own hands. You may use building blocks
(layers, optimizers, data loaders), but not finished models.

- You MAY NOT use any pre-built VAE, GAN, or diffusion library for the core model
  (no PyTorch Lightning VAE helpers, no torchgan, no diffusers UNet2DModel for the denoiser).
- You MUST implement the reparameterization trick, ELBO computation, noise schedule, and
  DDPM sampling loop manually.
- Your diffusion denoiser must be your own architecture — a small U-Net or MLP you define
  yourself. You MAY use `nn.Conv2d`, `nn.Linear`, etc.
- You MUST use the same dataset and the same train/test split for all three models.
- You MAY use `torchmetrics` for FID/KID computation.
- You MAY use `torch.optim.Adam` and standard DataLoaders.

No AI assistants writing the model code, either: the skills checklist at the end only means
something if you wrote and debugged the code yourself.

> **Watch out:** `torchmetrics` FID needs the `torch-fidelity` package and downloads Inception
> weights (about 91 MB) the first time. Install it early, and run one tiny FID end-to-end
> before training anything, so a missing dependency doesn't block you at the end.

## What it must contain (and the chapter each tests)

Each bullet is one piece of the project, with the chapter that taught it. Build them in this
order: VAE first (fastest feedback), then the noise schedule, then DDPM, then the GAN.

- **VAE** (ch. 3): encoder $q_\phi(z|x)$ outputs $\mu$ and $\log\sigma^2$; reparameterization
  $z = \mu + \sigma \odot \epsilon$, $\epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$; ELBO loss =
  reconstruction + closed-form KL; sample from the prior $z \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$ and
  decode; interpolate between two latent codes and show the smooth transition.
  In plain words: encode to a bell curve per image, sample with the reparameterization trick,
  train on reconstruction plus the KL tax, then generate by decoding random codes. For the
  **interpolation**, encode two test images, walk in 8–10 even steps along the straight line
  between their codes, and decode each step.
- **DDPM — noise schedule** (ch. 7): implement both a linear schedule ($\beta_1 = 10^{-4}$
  to $\beta_T = 0.02$, $T = 1000$) and a cosine schedule; precompute $\alpha_t$, $\bar\alpha_t$,
  and the closed-form $q(x_t|x_0)$ for arbitrary $t$.
  In plain words: build both noise recipes, store the per-step and cumulative signal shares,
  and write the one-line "jump to step $t$" function.
- **DDPM — training and sampling** (ch. 8): simplified loss
  $\mathcal{L} = \|\epsilon - \epsilon_\theta(x_t, t)\|^2$; reverse sampling loop
  $x_{t-1} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_t}}(x_t - \frac{1-\alpha_t}{\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}}\epsilon_\theta(x_t,t)) + \sigma_t z$;
  sample a grid of images from noise.
  In plain words: train a time-aware network to guess the added noise, then run the 1000-step
  reverse loop from pure noise. Note $1 - \alpha_t = \beta_t$, so this is the same update as ch. 8.
- **GAN** (ch. 4–5): DCGAN-style generator and discriminator; non-saturating generator
  loss $-\mathbb{E}[\log D(G(z))]$; alternating update; at least 20 epochs of training;
  show generated samples.
  In plain words: a convolutional forger and inspector, trained in turns, with the generator
  loss that keeps learning even when it's losing.
- **Evaluation** (ch. 11): compute FID or KID on 1 000+ generated images vs the test set
  for each model; report in a comparison table.
  In plain words: one number per model, measured the same way. MNIST is greyscale, so repeat
  the channel three times before feeding Inception.
- **Tradeoff analysis** (written, no code required): compare VAE vs GAN vs DDPM on all
  four dimensions: sample quality, training stability, exact likelihood availability,
  sampling speed (seconds per image or batch).
  In plain words: a short report, backed by your own numbers, on what each model is good and
  bad at.

> **Watch out:** DDPM sampling with 1000 steps is slow on a CPU. Debug with $T = 200$ or with
> a few samples, and generate the 1,000+ evaluation images only once, at the end. Time it — the
> number goes into the speed comparison.

## Acceptance criteria

These are the tests your project must pass. Each can be checked by looking at a plot, a grid of
images, or a table.

- VAE ELBO decreases monotonically and converges; ELBO on the test set is reported.
- VAE samples from the prior are recognizable as belonging to the training distribution
  (not random noise); latent interpolation shows smooth transitions.
- DDPM sampling from random Gaussian noise produces recognizable images after the full
  reverse chain; show a grid of at least 16 samples.
- GAN trains without complete mode collapse: generated samples show variety across at
  least 5 distinct classes or modes.
- FID or KID is reported for all three models on the same dataset and test split.
- The tradeoff analysis addresses: which model produces the best FID, which is most
  stable to train, which gives exact NLL, which is fastest to sample from.

For the first item, read "ELBO decreases" as "the negative-ELBO *loss* decreases" (the ELBO
itself rises) — per epoch, apart from small mini-batch noise.

> **Watch out:** report every FID with how it was computed: feature layer (2048 for comparable
> numbers), number of generated images, and which test images formed the reference set. FIDs
> from 1,000 images are biased upward, so compare your three models with each other, not with
> published numbers.

> **Watch out:** "which gives exact NLL" is a trick in the best sense: of your three models,
> none gives an exact NLL. The VAE gives a bound (the ELBO), the DDPM gives a bound too, and the
> GAN gives nothing. Normalizing flows (ch. 6) and autoregressive models (ch. 10) are the exact
> ones; say so in your analysis.

## Skills checklist (tick before you claim done)

Tick a box only if you could do it again tomorrow, without notes.

- [ ] I can implement the reparameterization trick in a single line and explain why
  the stop-gradient is necessary for backpropagation. (More precisely: why the sampling step
  must be rewritten as $\mu + \sigma \odot \epsilon$ so gradients reach $\mu$ and $\sigma$.)
- [ ] I can compute the ELBO analytically (closed-form KL for Gaussian prior) and
  verify the reconstruction term matches MSE or BCE depending on output distribution.
- [ ] I can implement the linear and cosine noise schedules and plot $\bar\alpha_t$ vs
  $t$ to confirm the cosine schedule is smoother.
- [ ] I can implement the DDPM forward process $q(x_t|x_0)$ in closed form and
  verify by comparing to iterative noising.
- [ ] I can implement the DDPM simplified training loss and a time-conditioned denoising network.
- [ ] I can implement the DDPM reverse sampling loop and generate images from noise.
- [ ] I can implement the GAN minimax training loop and explain why non-saturating
  loss fixes the vanishing-gradient problem of the original minimax.
- [ ] I can diagnose mode collapse from generated samples and name one architectural
  or loss-based fix.
- [ ] I can compute FID with `torchmetrics` and explain what the Fréchet distance measures.
- [ ] I can write a crisp tradeoff analysis that explains why diffusion has tractable NLL
  while GANs do not, and why GANs typically sample faster than diffusion.
