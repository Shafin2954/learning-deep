## The project

Build a complete recommender for one real interaction log — MovieLens, a public e-commerce or
music-listening log, or your own product's events — and evaluate it the way a production team
would. The stack has a retrieval stage fed by several candidate sources (matrix factorisation
trained two ways, a two-tower model, and a sequential model), a learning-to-rank stage that reorders
the merged candidates, a cold-start path for users and items with no history, and an offline
off-policy estimate of how a bandit-style exploration policy would have done. Every component is
measured under a temporal split against honest baselines, and the deliverable is the code plus a
report that says which parts earned their complexity and which did not. "The neural model wins" is
not a required outcome; a measured "it did not, and here is why" is a perfectly good capstone.

## Hard constraints (no vibe coding)

- **No AI assistants** for the code, the modelling decisions, or the report.
- **From scratch** (NumPy/SciPy/PyTorch only): ALS for explicit feedback, implicit ALS (iALS),
  BPR, the two-tower model with in-batch negatives and the logQ correction, the sequential model
  (GRU4Rec or SASRec), the ranking losses, every metric, and the off-policy estimators.
- **No recommender libraries** (implicit, LightFM, Surprise, RecBole, TorchRec, Merlin) in the
  submitted pipeline — you may use one only inside a test that checks your implementation.
- **No random splits for the headline numbers.** Every reported metric uses a temporal split.
- **Full-ranking evaluation** for the headline numbers: rank against the whole catalogue, not
  against a sampled set of negatives. A sampled-metric number may appear only next to its
  full-ranking counterpart.
- **Every number in the report comes from a script in the repository**, with fixed seeds.

## What it must contain (and the chapter each tests)

1. **A problem statement and data audit**: explicit vs implicit signal, activity and popularity
   long tails (with the Gini coefficient of item popularity), and the share of test interactions
   that involve cold users and cold items. (ch. 1)
2. **An evaluation harness** with a temporal split, recall@k, NDCG@k, MAP@k, catalogue coverage,
   and per-user bootstrap confidence intervals, validated against hand-computed toy cases. (ch. 2)
3. **Baselines that are hard to beat**: global popularity, recent popularity, and item–item
   neighbourhood CF with a tuned shrinkage term. (ch. 3)
4. **Matrix factorisation two ways**: explicit ALS with biases on ratings (if the data has them),
   and implicit-feedback models — iALS with a confidence weight and BPR with sampled negatives —
   tuned on a validation window, not on test. (ch. 4–5)
5. **A factorisation machine** using at least two side features, with its $O(kn)$ reformulation
   implemented and checked against the naive $O(kn^2)$ version. (ch. 6)
6. **A two-tower retrieval model** with in-batch softmax, the logQ popularity correction (with and
   without, reported), and item features in the item tower. (ch. 7)
7. **A sequential model** (GRU4Rec or SASRec) evaluated under leave-last-out *and* under the
   temporal split, with the difference between the two protocols explained. (ch. 8)
8. **A cold-start path**: a content-based or attribute-to-factor model for new items and an
   onboarding or segment-popularity strategy for new users, evaluated on entity-held-out users and
   items, not on random interactions. (ch. 9)
9. **A two-stage pipeline**: merged candidates from at least three sources, candidate recall@N
   reported as the ceiling, and a learning-to-rank model (pairwise or LambdaRank) trained on a
   time-shifted table so that no label leaks into a feature; plus one re-ranking rule (MMR or a
   diversity cap) with its accuracy cost reported. (ch. 10)
10. **An exploration and off-policy study**: a LinUCB or Thompson-sampling policy run in a
    simulator fitted to your data, and IPS, SNIPS and doubly-robust estimates of a new policy's
    value computed from logged data, with their variance compared. (ch. 11)

## Acceptance criteria

- **Leak-free by construction.** A test shows that no training interaction has a timestamp after the
  split point, and that every ranker feature is computed from data strictly before its label window.
- **Baselines reported first.** Every model's NDCG@10 and recall@10 appear in one table next to
  popularity and item–item CF, with bootstrap 95% intervals; claims of improvement are made only
  where the intervals do not overlap.
- **Your MF is correct.** On a small synthetic matrix with known low-rank structure, your ALS and
  iALS recover held-out entries at least as well as a reference library run inside a test.
- **The two stages are honest.** The final NDCG@10 of retrieval + ranking is reported together
  with the candidate-recall ceiling and the NDCG@10 of retrieval alone, at three or more
  candidate-set sizes.
- **Cold start is measured separately.** The report has warm, cold-user and cold-item columns, and
  the cold-start path beats popularity on at least one cold column.
- **Off-policy estimates are checked.** In the simulator, where the true value of the new policy is
  known, your doubly-robust estimate lands within two of its own standard errors of the truth.
- **Beyond accuracy.** Catalogue coverage and the share of recommendations from the long-tail half
  of the catalogue are reported for every model.
- **Reproducible.** One command regenerates every table in the report from the raw log.

## Skills checklist (tick before you claim done)

- [ ] I can frame a recommendation problem as explicit or implicit feedback and say what the label really is.
- [ ] I can build a temporal evaluation with full-ranking NDCG, recall, MAP and bootstrap intervals.
- [ ] I can implement item–item CF with shrinkage and explain why it is a strong baseline.
- [ ] I can derive and implement ALS, iALS and BPR, and say when each fits the data.
- [ ] I can implement a factorisation machine in linear time and explain what it adds over MF.
- [ ] I can train a two-tower model with in-batch negatives and explain the logQ correction.
- [ ] I can build and evaluate a sequential recommender without falling into the sampled-metrics trap.
- [ ] I can design a cold-start strategy and evaluate it on genuinely unseen users and items.
- [ ] I can build a candidate-generation + ranking pipeline and train the ranker without leakage.
- [ ] I can run a contextual bandit and estimate a new policy's value from logged data with IPS and DR.
- [ ] I can report diversity and coverage alongside accuracy, and argue for the trade-off I chose.
