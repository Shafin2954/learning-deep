## The map

Before you train anything, you pick a starting model that already knows some Bengali. That
choice is mostly about three questions: what shape of task you have, how well the model
knows Bengali, and whether it fits on your GPU.

You now understand **encoders** (models that read the whole input at once to understand it),
**decoders** (models that write text one token at a time), and **encoder-decoders** (a reader
plus a writer, for turning one text into another) (F12). Choosing your base model *is*
choosing among those three, filtered by Bengali coverage, fertility (ch. 3), size (your 8GB
card), and **license** (the legal terms for using the weights). Pick wrong and you fight
the model all month; pick right and fine-tuning is easy. Here's the map. (Exact "best"
models shift monthly — verify current options on the Hugging Face hub and any Bengali
leaderboard before committing; treat the names below as the stable landmarks.)

> **Picture it:** hiring for a job. An encoder is a careful reader who marks up documents.
> A decoder is a writer who continues whatever you start. An encoder-decoder is a
> translator who reads a page and writes a new one. Hire for the job you actually have.

## Encoder models: understanding tasks

Encoders are the "read and judge" models: they look at every word at once, in both
directions, and output a label or a vector, not new text.

**Bidirectional** (each token can look at tokens before *and* after it), no **causal mask**
(the rule that hides future tokens) — best for classification, sentiment, **NLI** (deciding
whether one sentence follows from another), **NER** (tagging names of people, places, and
organizations), extractive QA, and producing **embeddings** (number vectors that capture
meaning, ch. 5).

- **BanglaBERT** (csebuetnlp) — ELECTRA-style, trained on a large Bengali corpus with the
  csebuetnlp normalizer (ch. 2). Low Bengali fertility, the standard strong baseline for
  Bengali understanding tasks. Small enough to **full-fine-tune** (update every weight) on 8GB.
- **MuRIL** (Google) — 17 Indian languages incl. Bengali, trained with **transliteration**
  pairs (the same word in native and Latin script); strong on code-mixed/transliterated
  Bengali ("Banglish").
- **IndicBERT** (AI4Bharat), **XLM-RoBERTa** (multilingual, 100 langs) — solid
  multilingual fallbacks; XLM-R-large is a heavier but reliable choice.

> **Watch out:** "ELECTRA-style" changes the training recipe, not how you use the model.
> You still load BanglaBERT with the ordinary `AutoModelFor...` classes (ch. 7).

## Decoder / generative LLMs: generation tasks

Decoders are the "write the next word" models. Anything where the answer is free text,
not a label, lives here.

**Causal** (each token sees only the tokens before it) — for open QA, chat, summarization,
reasoning, instruction following.

- **TituLLM / TituLM** (Hishab, 2025) — Bengali-focused LLMs ($\approx$1B–3B),
  continue-trained for Bengali; good size/quality fit for your hardware via QLoRA.
- **TigerLLM** (2025) — another recent Bengali-centric LLM line.
- **Multilingual general LLMs with real Bengali coverage:** Llama-3.x (8B), Qwen2.5
  (varied sizes), Gemma-2, Aya (Cohere, explicitly multilingual), BLOOM. These are strong
  and well-supported by the fine-tuning stack (PEFT/Unsloth, ch. 9), but check Bengali
  fertility and quality — general models vary a lot on Bengali.

> **Watch out:** model names on the Hub carry version suffixes. The TituLM 1B checkpoint is
> published as `hishab/titulm-llama-3.2-1b-v2.0` (base) and
> `hishab/titulm-llama-3.2-1b-v2.0-Instruct-v1.0` (instruction-tuned). A shortened name such
> as `hishab/titulm-1b` fails to load. Copy the exact ID from the model page.

## Encoder-decoder: seq2seq tasks

**Seq2seq** means "sequence to sequence": text goes in, a different text comes out.

For translation and summarization where input and output are both text.

- **BanglaT5** (csebuetnlp) — Bengali seq2seq, the natural choice for Bn$\leftrightarrow$En
  translation and Bengali summarization.
- **mT5 / IndicBART** — multilingual seq2seq fallbacks.

## How to actually choose

The plain idea: write your candidates in a small table, then knock them out in a fixed
order, starting with the question that rules out the most.

```python
# A model-selection scorecard you fill in for the competition (no model load needed).
# Run it as a checklist; replace the placeholder numbers with measured ones.
candidates = [
    # name,                arch,      params,  bengali_native, full_ft_on_8gb
    ("csebuetnlp/banglabert", "encoder",  110,   True,  True),
    ("csebuetnlp/banglat5",   "enc-dec",  220,   True,  True),
    ("hishab/titulm-llama-3.2-1b-v2.0", "decoder", 1000,   True,  False),   # QLoRA only
    ("Qwen2.5-7B",            "decoder", 7000,   False, False),   # QLoRA only
]
print(f"{'model':32s} {'arch':9s} {'params(M)':>9s} {'native':>7s} {'fullFT8GB':>10s}")
for name, arch, p, native, fullft in candidates:
    print(f"{name:32s} {arch:9s} {p:>9d} {str(native):>7s} {str(fullft):>10s}")
# Decision rule from the chapter: task -> arch first; then native+low-fertility;
# then size vs 8GB (full-FT small, QLoRA big); then license/offline; start small.
```

```output
model                            arch      params(M)  native  fullFT8GB
csebuetnlp/banglabert            encoder         110    True       True
csebuetnlp/banglat5              enc-dec         220    True       True
hishab/titulm-llama-3.2-1b-v2.0  decoder        1000    True      False
Qwen2.5-7B                       decoder        7000   False      False
```

The table makes the trade-off visible. The two csebuetnlp models are native Bengali and small
(110M and 220M parameters), so both say `True` for full fine-tuning on 8GB. The two decoders
(1000M and 7000M) say `False`: they only fit with QLoRA (ch. 9). The parameter counts here are
rounded placeholders; replace them with the numbers from each model card.

The decision order:

1. **Task family decides the architecture.** Understanding $\to$ encoder. Generation $\to$
   decoder. Text-in/text-out transduction (translate/summarize) $\to$ enc-dec (or a
   decoder LLM prompted to do it). This is the single biggest fork; get it right first
   (ch. 7).
2. **Bengali coverage + fertility (ch. 3).** Prefer Bengali-specific or Indic models;
   measure fertility on your data. High fertility = truncation + cost.
3. **Size vs your 8GB card.** Full fine-tune: stick to $\le \approx 300$M (BanglaBERT
   fits). Bigger (1B–8B): QLoRA only (ch. 9). A 7–8B model in 4-bit is the ceiling on
   8GB.
4. **License + reproducibility + Kaggle availability.** The model must be usable under the
   competition rules and loadable in the (often offline) submission environment (ch. 13).
   Check the license and that weights can be pre-downloaded as a Kaggle dataset.
5. **Start small, prove the loop, then scale.** A small encoder baseline you can iterate
   on in minutes beats a 7B model you can train once a day. Earn the right to go big.

> **In plain words:** "$\le \approx 300$M" reads "at most about 300 million parameters". In
> full fine-tuning each parameter needs its weight, its gradient, and two optimizer numbers,
> so memory grows several times faster than the model file size.

> **Picture it:** a funnel. The task drops most models in one go; Bengali coverage drops more;
> GPU size and license drop the rest. Whatever is left at the bottom is your shortlist.

The honest default for week 1: understanding task $\to$ **BanglaBERT**, full fine-tune.
Generation task $\to$ a small Bengali or multilingual decoder via **QLoRA**. Translation/
summarization $\to$ **BanglaT5**. Get a submitted baseline with one of these before you
chase anything fancier (ch. 17).

## Recap in plain words

- Encoders understand (labels, tags, spans, embeddings); decoders generate; encoder-decoders turn one text into another.
- BanglaBERT is the default for understanding tasks, BanglaT5 for translation and summarization.
- TituLM, TigerLLM, and multilingual LLMs (Llama, Qwen, Gemma, Aya) cover generation, usually through QLoRA.
- Choose in order: task shape, Bengali coverage and fertility, size on 8GB, license and offline loading.
- Under about 300M parameters you can full fine-tune on 8GB; 1B to 8B needs 4-bit QLoRA.
- Start with the small model you can iterate on in minutes.
